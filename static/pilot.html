KASUNGU DISTRICT · ONE SEASON

Eleven weeks, three officers, 900 farms

The pilot tests one claim: surveying the farms whose answers change Felix’s day finds crop problems sooner than routine visits.

Photo: USAID Mozambique, smallholder fields, public domain

STEPS AND OWNERS

Who does what, and when

WEEKS 1–2

Set up

  • Register the /ussd callback with a USSD code from Africa’s Talking.
  • Host Fieldwise on one small cloud server.
  • Confirm data protection duties with MACRA under Malawi’s Data Protection Act 2024.

Owner: Fieldwise team

WEEK 2

Enrol and train

  • Enrol 300 of each officer’s farms (Malawi averages about 2,000 per worker) with farm numbers and coordinates only.
  • Two-hour session per officer: ranking, surveys, recording outcomes.
  • Farmers get their farm number and the USSD code on a printed card.

Owner: Kasungu District Agriculture Office (proposed partner, not yet contacted)

WEEKS 3–10

Run

  • Officers follow the ranked list and the “who to ask next” queue.
  • Every visit ends with a tapped outcome, which trains the model.
  • Half the officers’ farms use the ranking; half keep routine visits, for comparison.

Owners: extension officers; Fieldwise team on call

WEEK 11

Decide

  • Compare problem-finding rates between ranked and routine farms.
  • Continue and expand if ranked visits confirm problems more often and sooner.
  • Delete pilot data the plan no longer needs.

Owners: district office and Fieldwise team
